# Makita Contact Plates


> Using Pre-Made Contact Plates for Makita LTX Batteries

Contact plates are the core of most adapters, and in its most simplistic form, they feature just two metal contact rails that slide into the battery.    

You can source these plates for cheap at *AliExpress*, or [3D-print them yourself](https://grabcad.com/library/makita-18v-battery-connector-1/files). 

> [!NOTE]
> The "most valuable" part of these adapters are the metal contacts, so if you 3D-print these plates, you'll still need the metal contacts, and if you are 3D-printing anyway, then why not [integrate the contacts directly](https://done.land/components/power/powersupplies/battery/toolbatteries/makita/custombatteryadapters/3dprintingadapters/integratedbasic/) into your design rather than using separate contact plates.

More advanced contact plates come with an additional third contact that provides analog thermal sensor information:

<img src="images/makita_contact_plate_2vs3_1_t.webp" width="50%" height="50%" />

The latter can be useful for high-current use cases where you need to keep an eye on the battery temperature, yet in most scenarios the two-contact plate is sufficient.

> [!NOTE]
> Contact plates with three instead of two contacts are less common: they are significantly more costly. Avoid such plates unless you really need access to the battery temperature probe.
