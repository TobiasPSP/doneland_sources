# Cooling 

> Mandatory for many Power Electronics

Cooling is an essential aspect of power electronics: inevitable electrical losses generate heat that must be managed.

Effective thermal management involves transferring heat away from critical components and dissipating it into the surrounding environment. This is typically achieved with 

* **Heatsinks:**   
  *Passive* cooling by transferring punctual heat (i.e. from a MOSFET) to a larger thermal mass, often with fins to increase surface area and improve heat dissipation to the air.   
* **Thermal Paste/Pads:**      
  Improve heat *transfer* between the heat source and the sink by filling air gaps with highly temperature-conductive material.    
* **Fans:**     
  *Active* cooling by increasing air flow.
  
  
Proper cooling not only improves logevity (electronic components age faster at higher temperatures). Often, cooling is a non-negotiable prerequisite. Without it, components can go into protective thermal shutdown (reducing performance) or are permanently damaged.
